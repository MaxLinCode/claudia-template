# Habit Context: Stretch Rehab

## Why
To stay durable, mobile, and physically ready for badminton. This habit exists to support performance, reduce injury risk, and keep your body feeling playable even when training load or life stress is high.

## Rules of Engagement
- **Frequency:** Daily.
- **Minimum Viable Threshold:** 10 minutes of stretching or rehab work.
  - Acceptable categories:
    - Lower-body mobility
    - Shoulder or forearm rehab
    - Ankles, hips, calves, or hamstrings
    - Core stability or activation tied to badminton movement
- **Definition of Success:** ✅ if you complete 10 intentional minutes.
- **Definition of Skip:** ⏸️ if you are sick, traveling, or physically unable to do meaningful work.
- **Note Format:** Log the body area or routine. Example: "10m hip + calf mobility" or "12m shoulder rehab and band work."

## The Trigger
- **Primary:** Do it immediately after your first shower, workout, or badminton session of the day.
- **Fallback Trigger:** If that window gets missed, do it before bed.
- **Implementation Intention:** "After I finish my workout, badminton, or evening reset, I will do 10 minutes of stretch/rehab before I move on."

## Backup Protocols
- **If** energy is low, **Then** do the easiest 10-minute mobility sequence you already know.
- **If** soreness is high, **Then** switch from performance work to gentle recovery and range-of-motion work.
- **If** the day is chaotic, **Then** do one 10-minute floor routine before bed instead of skipping.

## Coaching Notes
- Treat this as athletic maintenance, not optional self-care.
- Consistency matters more than novelty.
- Watch for recurring notes on the same pain or tightness pattern; those are signals, not noise.
